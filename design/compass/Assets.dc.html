Asset rules — what a replacement has to satisfy
Measured from the shipped files and the code that draws them, not assumed. Anything here that a new asset breaks, breaks silently in game.
The rotation safe zone
GW2 mode free-rotates the arrow with an 8-corner SetTexCoord. The sampled quad always contains the texture's inscribed circle and never more, so art inside the circle is safe at every bearing and the four corners are cut at some of them. Draw to the circle, not the square.
Second rule, and the one today's arrow misses: the art must be centred on the texture, because rotation is about the texture's midpoint, not the art's. Today's ink spans y 10–229 of 256, so its centre sits 8px high and the arrow orbits by about 1.4px at the shipped 44px size as it turns.
Shipped files and sizes
arrow-gw2.tga256×256, free-rotated. Art must fit r=128 and be centred. Drawn at 44px.
arrow.tga512×512 sheet, 9×12 cells of 56×42 = 108 bearings. Standalone only. Drawn at 28px.
marker_fill / _edge64×64 each, white, tinted at runtime. Drawn at 20px with a 12px icon.
marker_glow64×64, ADD-blended halo behind the plate.
beam_soft.tga32×256, horizontal gaussian, vertically uniform — stretched to any beam height.
compass strip420×22 default, 1×6 ticks, 20px plates, 7px badge, 12px description line.
Draw white, tint at runtime
Every plate and beam texture ships white and is coloured with SetVertexColor. That is what lets one file serve GW2 gold and standalone teal. A coloured asset forks into two files and drifts.
TGA only, power of two
3.3.5a reads no PNG. Sizes stay powers of two (64, 256, 512); a 20px plate is a 64px texture scaled down, never a 20px file.
One arrow could replace two
The 8-corner rotation needs no sprite sheet. A single centred 256×256 arrow can serve standalone as well, deleting arrow.tga and its 108 hand-rendered cells — 220 KB, and one less thing to redraw next time.
What has no art cost
Compass B (Ruler) and Marker D (Bare) ship no new textures at all — they remove drawing rather than add it. If the goal is a better fit rather than a new look, start there.
